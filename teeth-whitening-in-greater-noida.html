Why Teeth Lose Their Shine

Teeth are naturally a shade of off-white, and they darken over time. Tea, coffee, cola, red wine and curry leave pigments on the surface. Tobacco and betel (paan) stain strongly. Age thins the enamel, which lets the yellower layer underneath show through. Some medicines, an old injury or a root canal treated tooth can darken a single tooth from the inside.

Knowing the cause matters, because surface stains respond well to cleaning and whitening, while stains from inside the tooth may need a different approach. That is why we examine your teeth before we suggest any whitening treatment.

Professional Whitening vs Over-the-Counter Products

Over-the-counter strips and pastes are weaker, and their results vary. Some are abrasive and can wear the enamel if used too often. Professional whitening is carried out or supervised by a dentist, uses carefully controlled products, protects the gums and gives a more predictable result.

Before treatment, we check for cavities, gum disease and cracks, because whitening agents can cause pain if they reach exposed dentine. If anything needs treating, we fix it first. We also tell you honestly how much improvement to expect, because every tooth responds differently.

What Happens During a Whitening Visit

  • We examine your teeth and record your starting shade.
  • We clean the teeth to remove plaque and surface stains.
  • The gums and lips are protected before the whitening gel is applied.
  • The gel is left on for a set time and then removed.
  • We compare the shade and advise you on aftercare.

Some people feel mild sensitivity for a day or two afterwards. Tell us if you already have sensitive teeth, and we will adjust the plan.

Keeping Your Teeth White

For the first couple of days after whitening, avoid strongly coloured food and drink such as tea, coffee, red wine, curry and tobacco, because the teeth are more receptive to stains. After that, rinse your mouth with water after dark drinks, use a straw for cold coffee and keep up with brushing and cleaning between your teeth.

A professional clean every six months removes new surface stains and helps the result last longer. Results vary from person to person, depending on diet and habits.

What Whitening Cannot Do

Whitening only changes natural tooth surfaces. It cannot change the colour of crowns, veneers or fillings, so if you have these, we plan around them and may suggest replacing an old restoration after whitening so the shades match.

It also cannot straighten teeth, close gaps or repair chips. If your smile has those issues, whitening may brighten it but not complete it. Sensitivity and Safety

Mild sensitivity after whitening is common and usually settles within a day or two. To reduce it, we check your teeth first, protect your gums, choose a suitable strength and may recommend a desensitising toothpaste. If you have worn enamel, exposed roots or cracked teeth, we may advise against whitening or treat those first.

Whitening is not recommended during pregnancy or for young children, and we will not carry it out on unhealthy teeth or gums. If you are not sure whether you are suitable, a short examination will tell us. Our advice is always to put the health of your teeth first.

Habits That Stain Your Teeth

Knowing what stains your teeth helps you to protect your result. The main causes are tea, coffee, cola and red wine; tobacco and paan; turmeric-rich or heavily coloured curries; and some mouthwashes and medicines. Poor brushing lets plaque hold stains on the surface.

Small changes help: rinse with water after coloured drinks, use a straw for iced coffee and brush gently twice a day. Cutting down on tobacco is good for your teeth and your whole body, and we are happy to talk about it.

Questions About Whitening

How white will my teeth get? It depends on your starting shade and cause of stain. Yellow-toned teeth usually respond well, and grey or brown tones may respond less. Can I whiten if I have gum disease? We treat gum disease first. Is it safe for enamel? Done under supervision on healthy teeth, yes.

Can I whiten more than once? Often yes, with some time between treatments, and we advise on how often. What about whitening toothpaste? It removes surface stains but does not change the natural shade much. Do natural remedies work? Most do not whiten and some, like lemon juice, can damage enamel.
