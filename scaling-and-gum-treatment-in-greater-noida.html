Plaque, Tartar and Why Brushing Is Not Enough

Plaque is a sticky film of bacteria that forms on your teeth every day. If it is not removed, it hardens into tartar, also called calculus, which a toothbrush cannot remove. Tartar gives bacteria a rough surface to grow on and irritates the gums, which then swell and bleed.

Even with excellent brushing, some deposits form in places that are hard to reach, such as behind the lower front teeth and between the back teeth. A professional clean removes them and leaves the teeth smooth and easier to keep clean.

Gum Disease: From Gingivitis to Periodontitis

Early gum disease, called gingivitis, shows as red, swollen gums that bleed when you brush or floss. It can usually be reversed with professional cleaning and good home care. If it is ignored, it can progress to periodontitis, where the bone that supports the teeth begins to be lost.

Signs of advanced gum disease include gums that pull away from the teeth, persistent bad breath, pus, and teeth that feel loose or look longer. This stage needs deeper treatment, and we explain what is involved after examining your gums.

What Happens During Scaling and Polishing

We check your gums and measure the depth of the pockets around the teeth. Scaling then removes plaque and tartar above and, where needed, below the gum line. We then polish the teeth to remove surface stains and leave them smooth.

Your gums may be slightly tender and may bleed a little for a day or two afterwards. This settles as the inflammation reduces. Teeth may also feel a little sensitive at first. We show you how to brush and clean between your teeth effectively, and when to come back.

Bad Breath and Bleeding Gums

Persistent bad breath most often comes from the mouth, usually from plaque on the teeth, the gums and the tongue. Bleeding gums are a sign of inflammation, not a reason to brush less. Gentle, thorough cleaning, together with professional scaling, usually improves both.

If your breath stays bad despite good cleaning, tell us. We check for gum disease, decay, a dry mouth and other causes, and advise you on the next step.

Daily Home Care That Works

  • Brush twice a day for two minutes with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean between your teeth once a day with floss or interdental brushes.
  • Clean your tongue gently to reduce bacteria.
  • Limit sugary snacks and drinks between meals.
  • Do not smoke or chew tobacco, which damage the gums.
  • Book a check-up and clean about every six months, or as often as we advise.

If you wear braces, interdental brushes and a water flosser, if recommended by your dentist, make cleaning easier.

Gums frame your teeth, so the health and shape of the gum line shape your smile. Swollen or red gums make teeth look shorter and less even, while healthy pink gums make them look brighter.

Who Is Most at Risk of Gum Disease?

Anyone can get gum disease, but some people are more at risk. Smoking and tobacco chewing are the biggest risk factors, because they reduce blood flow to the gums and hide the signs of disease. Diabetes makes gum disease more likely and harder to control. Pregnancy and some medicines change the gums, and a family history can play a part.

Crowded teeth are harder to clean, so plaque collects between them. If you have crowded teeth, straightening may make home cleaning easier, which is one reason people with gum trouble ask about braces treatment in Greater Noida and clear aligners in Greater Noida once their gums are healthy.

Deeper Cleaning and Ongoing Care

If gum disease has progressed, we may recommend a deeper clean below the gum line, sometimes in more than one visit, with local anaesthesia for comfort. We then review how your gums respond. Gum disease cannot be cured in the way a cavity can be filled, but it can be controlled with good home care and regular professional visits.

We will tell you how often to return, which is often every three to six months for people with a history of gum disease. Sticking to this schedule is the best way to keep your teeth for life.

Gums During Pregnancy, Diabetes and Other Health Conditions

Hormones in pregnancy can make gums swollen and sensitive, so regular cleaning is especially useful. Tell us if you are pregnant before any treatment. Diabetes affects gums, and poorly controlled gum disease can make blood sugar harder to control, so gum care is part of managing diabetes.

Some medicines cause dry mouth or gum overgrowth. If you take regular medicines or have a long-term health condition, tell us so that we can plan your care and give you the right advice.

Receding Gums and Sensitive Teeth

Gums can recede from brushing too hard, gum disease, grinding or the way your teeth sit. When the root surface is exposed, teeth become sensitive to cold and sweet things and can decay more easily. We check the cause and advise on technique, toothpaste and, where needed, treatment.

Questions about scaling & gum treatment

How often should I get my teeth cleaned?

Many people do well with a professional clean every six months. If you have gum disease, we may recommend more frequent visits.

Does scaling damage the teeth?

No. Scaling removes deposits from the surface and does not harm healthy enamel. Teeth may feel slightly sensitive for a day or two.

Why do my gums bleed?

Most often because of plaque and gum inflammation. Bleeding is a sign to get checked, not a reason to brush less.

Can gum disease be treated?

Early gum disease can usually be reversed with professional cleaning and good home care. More advanced disease needs deeper treatment, which we will explain.
