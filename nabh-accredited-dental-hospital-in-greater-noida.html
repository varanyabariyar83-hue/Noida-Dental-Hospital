What Is NABH Accreditation?

NABH stands for the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers. It was set up in 2005 and is a constituent board of the Quality Council of India, which is itself an autonomous body set up by the Government of India together with Indian industry. NABH sets standards for healthcare organisations and assesses whether they meet them.

Accreditation means that the hospital has voluntarily asked to be assessed against these published standards by trained assessors, and has been found to meet them. It is different from a licence. A licence says a hospital is allowed to operate. Accreditation says that, over and above the legal minimum, the hospital has shown that it follows good practice in how it treats patients and runs its facility.

NABH has different programmes for different types of organisations, including full hospital accreditation, small healthcare organisations, dental healthcare service providers, and entry-level certification. That is why you will see the words NABH accredited used for a hospital, a dental hospital or a dental clinic.

What Does NABH Check?

The standards look at the whole patient experience, not only the treatment itself. Areas covered include:

  • Patient rights, information and informed consent
  • Patient safety and correct identification of patients
  • Infection prevention and control, including sterilisation of instruments
  • Safe use and storage of medicines and materials
  • Qualifications, training and conduct of the clinical team
  • Maintaining clear medical records
  • Safety of the building, equipment and emergency preparedness
  • Handling of complaints and continuous improvement

None of these is glamorous, but all of them affect you. A hospital that works to these standards has written procedures, trained staff and regular checks, and it is expected to keep improving.

How a Hospital Gets NABH Accredited

The process is demanding, which is why accreditation means something.

  • The hospital applies to NABH for the relevant programme and studies the standards.
  • It prepares, trains its staff, puts written procedures in place and carries out its own assessment.
  • Trained NABH assessors visit, observe the work, review records and speak to staff.
  • NABH reviews the findings and decides whether to grant accreditation.
  • The accreditation is granted for a fixed period, and the hospital must keep meeting the standards and apply for renewal to retain it.

It is not a one-time certificate on the wall. The hospital has to keep working to the standards, and it can be assessed again.

Frequently asked questions

What does NABH stand for?

NABH stands for the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers, a constituent board of the Quality Council of India.

Is NABH accreditation the same as a licence?

No. A licence allows a hospital to operate. Accreditation is a voluntary, independent assessment that the hospital follows recognised standards of quality and patient safety.

How can I check if a hospital is NABH accredited?

Ask the hospital for its certificate, check the number and validity dates, and look for it in the directory of accredited organisations on the NABH website.

Does accreditation guarantee treatment results?

No. It shows that the hospital follows good standards of safety and process. No honest dentist can guarantee results.
