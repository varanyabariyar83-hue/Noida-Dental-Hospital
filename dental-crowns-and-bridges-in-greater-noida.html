Dental Crowns & Bridges in Greater Noida

A crown covers and protects a weak or broken tooth. A bridge fills the gap left by a missing tooth. Both are made to match the shade and shape of your natural teeth.

What Is a Dental Crown?

A crown, sometimes called a cap, is a tooth-shaped covering that fits over a damaged or weak tooth. It restores the tooth’s shape, size and strength, and it protects what remains of the natural tooth. We recommend crowns for teeth with large fillings, teeth that have had root canal treatment, cracked or worn teeth and teeth that are badly shaped or discoloured.

A well-made crown matches the colour of your other teeth and fits the bite so that chewing feels natural.

What Is a Dental Bridge?

A bridge replaces one or more missing teeth by joining an artificial tooth to crowns on the teeth either side of the gap. It is a fixed solution, which you cannot remove, and it restores your ability to chew and to speak clearly.

A bridge relies on the neighbouring teeth, which need to be reshaped to hold it. That is why we check that those teeth are healthy and strong enough. If they are not, we will talk about alternatives such as an implant.

Crown and Bridge Materials

Common choices include ceramic and zirconia crowns, which look the most natural, and porcelain fused to metal, which is strong and often more economical. Metal crowns are extremely durable and are sometimes used for back teeth where appearance matters less.

The best material depends on the position of the tooth, how hard you bite, whether you grind your teeth, the colour of neighbouring teeth and your budget. We explain the options and recommend one that suits your case.

The Crown or Bridge Process

  • Examination: we check the tooth, the gums and your bite, and take X-rays.
  • Preparation: the tooth is shaped under local anaesthesia, and an impression or scan is taken.
  • Temporary crown: a temporary crown protects the tooth while the final one is made.
  • Fitting: the final crown or bridge is checked for fit, bite and colour and then cemented.
  • Aftercare: regular cleaning around the crown keeps the gums healthy.

Most crowns need two visits. We explain the timeline at your first appointment.

Crowns vs Straightening: Choosing the Right Option

A crown is not always the best way to change the look of a crooked tooth. Cutting down a healthy tooth to fit a crown is a bigger step than moving it into place. When the problem is alignment, straightening with braces or aligners can protect your natural teeth. Your dentist will explain when a crown is the right choice and when straightening is.

Looking After Crowns and Bridges

Brush twice a day and clean around the crown or under the bridge with floss threaders or interdental brushes. Plaque at the edge of a crown can cause decay in the tooth underneath, so careful cleaning matters.

Avoid biting very hard objects, and tell us if you grind your teeth. If a crown feels loose, comes off, or the bite feels high, call us. A crown that is replaced early can usually be saved, while a neglected one may lead to decay or fracture of the tooth underneath.

Common Problems and How We Solve Them

Crowns can become loose, chipped or sensitive, and the tooth underneath can decay at the edge. A loose crown should be re-cemented quickly, ideally within a day or two, before the tooth is damaged. If a crown chips, we may be able to polish or repair it. Sensitivity after a new crown usually settles within a few weeks.

A bridge can loosen if one of the supporting teeth decays or the cement fails. Gum disease around a bridge can be hard to clean, which is why we show you how to clean under it. We see you for regular checks so that small problems do not become large ones.

Questions About Crowns and Bridges

Will a crown hurt? The tooth is numbed, and you feel pressure but not pain. How long does a crown last? It depends on your care, your bite and the material, and many last for years. Can I have a crown on a front tooth? Yes, and we match the shade carefully. Do I have to have the tooth cut down? Usually, some tooth is removed to make room for the crown, but we keep this to the minimum.

Can I have whitening after a crown? Whitening does not change the colour of crowns, so we plan whitening first and match the crown to the shade you like.

Signs a Tooth May Need a Crown

  • A large filling that takes up much of the tooth.
  • A crack that causes pain on biting or release.
  • A tooth that has had a root canal.
  • A worn-down tooth from grinding or acid.
  • A tooth that is badly discoloured or misshapen.
  • A tooth that is the support for a bridge or a denture clasp.

If you notice any of these, a check-up and X-ray will show whether a crown is the best option. Protecting a weakened tooth early often saves it from breaking.

Alternatives to Crowns and Bridges

Not every damaged tooth needs a crown. Small and medium cavities can be restored with fillings. A chipped front tooth may be repaired with bonding. For some tooth gaps, an implant avoids touching the neighbouring teeth, and a partial denture is a removable option. For crooked teeth, straightening may protect the natural tooth structure.

Questions about dental crowns & bridges

What are crowns made of?

Common options include ceramic and zirconia, and porcelain fused to metal. We recommend a material based on the tooth’s position, your bite and your budget.

How long does a crown last?

With good oral hygiene and regular check-ups, crowns can last many years. Habits such as grinding or chewing hard objects can shorten their life.

How is a bridge different from an implant?

A bridge uses the neighbouring teeth for support and is faster. An implant replaces the root and stands on its own. We help you compare the two.

Will the crown look natural?

Yes. We match the shade and shape to your surrounding teeth.
