Dental treatment is not one-size-fits-all, so we do not publish a single price for each procedure. The cost depends on your teeth, the complexity of the work and the materials used. What we do promise is clarity: after an examination, we give you an estimate that lists what is included, so you can decide with confidence.

Root Canals, Crowns, Bridges and Implants

These are treatments with several parts, so the final cost includes more than one item. A root canal includes the examination, X-rays, the root treatment and the filling, and usually a crown. A bridge or implant includes planning, the procedure and the final tooth. We list each part, so you can see what the estimate covers.

The type of crown and the number of teeth affect the price. For implants, the number of implants, the system used and any additional procedure such as bone grafting matter. We explain each one in your consultation.

Check-ups, Cleaning, Fillings and Whitening

Check-ups, cleaning, fillings and whitening are simple to quote once we have examined you. A filling depends on the size of the cavity and the material. Cleaning depends on how much tartar has built up. Whitening depends on the method and the condition of your teeth. We give you an estimate before we begin.

Regular check-ups are the cheapest dental care you can have, because they catch problems when they are small. Please do not put off a visit because you are worried about cost; call us, and we will tell you what to expect.

How to Compare Estimates Fairly

  • Ask each clinic for a written estimate.
  • Check what is included: records, X-rays, visits, materials and retainers.
  • Ask what happens if more treatment is needed.
  • Ask about the experience of the dentist doing the treatment.
  • Ask how long treatment will take and how many visits are needed.
  • Do not choose on price alone: the cheapest option can cost more if it fails.

Payment Options and Planning Ahead

Please ask us about payment options for your treatment plan. For longer treatment, we can discuss how it is planned in stages, so that you can spread the cost where that is possible. Planning ahead also helps: tell us your budget and your timeline, and we will build a plan that respects both.

Factors That Change the Price of Any Treatment

Several things affect cost across all treatments. The first is complexity: a tooth with simple decay costs less to fill than a tooth with a large cavity near the nerve. The second is materials: a ceramic crown costs more than a metal one, and a composite filling more than an older material. The third is time: a longer, more complex procedure takes more chairside time.

Other factors include how many visits are needed, whether specialised equipment or laboratory work is involved and whether you need preparatory treatment, such as gum care before braces. We explain which of these apply to you in your estimate.

Value Is More Than Price

The lowest price is not always the best value. A cheaper crown that fails in a few years costs more than one that lasts. Braces that are rushed can lead to relapse, and an implant that is badly planned may fail. Value comes from good diagnosis, good materials, careful work and follow-up care.

When you compare, ask what is included and how long it is expected to last.
