Common Causes of Toothache

Toothache has several possible causes, and the right treatment depends on which one you have. The most common are decay that has reached the nerve, a cracked tooth, an infection at the root, gum disease, a loose or broken filling, and a partly erupted wisdom tooth.

Sensitivity to cold or sweet food is often an early warning of decay or exposed root surface. Sharp pain on biting suggests a crack or a high filling. A deep, constant ache that keeps you up at night usually means the nerve is involved. A thorough examination and an X-ray let us find the real cause instead of guessing.

When Toothache Is an Emergency

Call us immediately if you have:

  • Swelling of the face, jaw or gum.
  • A fever together with tooth pain.
  • Pain that no tablet can control.
  • A tooth that has been knocked out or broken.
  • Bleeding that will not stop after a procedure.

Go to a hospital emergency department if you have difficulty breathing or swallowing. For everything else, call or WhatsApp us, tell us your symptoms and we will find you the earliest suitable time.

What You Can Do at Home Before You See Us

Rinse gently with lukewarm salt water to clean the area. Keep your head slightly raised when you lie down, because lying flat can increase the throbbing. A cold compress on the cheek helps with swelling. Take pain relief exactly as directed on the pack.

Do not place aspirin or any other tablet directly on the gum, because it can burn the tissue. Avoid very hot, cold and sweet food on the painful side. Home care eases symptoms for a while, but it does not treat the cause, so please do not wait for the pain to go away on its own.

How We Treat Toothache

Treatment starts with relieving the pain, then fixing the cause. Depending on what we find, this may mean removing decay and placing a filling, cleaning out an infection, treating the root canal, adjusting a high filling, treating the gums or removing a tooth that cannot be saved.

We explain the diagnosis and the options before we begin, and we give you an estimate for the full treatment. If you need a root canal, we will tell you what the procedure involves and how many visits are likely to be needed.

Preventing the Next Toothache

Most toothaches can be prevented. Brush for two minutes twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth daily, limit sugary snacks between meals and visit for a check-up and clean every six months. Small cavities are quick to fix, while a neglected one can end up needing a root canal.

If you wear braces or aligners, brush after every meal before putting the aligners back in, and use interdental brushes around brackets. If you grind your teeth, ask about a night guard. And if a filling or crown feels rough or loose, have it checked early.

Toothache Myths and Facts

A few beliefs cause real harm. Putting aspirin or clove on the gum is not safe or effective in the long run; aspirin can burn the gum, and clove oil only numbs briefly. A toothache that suddenly stops does not mean the problem has gone; it can mean the nerve has died and the infection is spreading. Antibiotics alone do not cure a tooth infection: they may calm it, but the tooth still needs treatment.

Another myth is that you should avoid the dentist when a tooth is swollen. In fact, a swollen face is a reason to be seen quickly. The sooner we see you, the simpler the treatment is likely to be.

Toothache in Children

Children often cannot tell us exactly what hurts. Look for signs such as refusing to eat, holding the cheek, crying at night, swelling or a dark tooth. A child with swelling or a fever should be seen the same day.

Do not wait for a milk tooth to fall out on its own if it is painful. Infection in a milk tooth can affect the adult tooth underneath. Our kids’ dentistry approach is gentle, and we explain what we are doing in simple words. After Emergency Treatment

Emergency treatment often relieves pain at the first visit, but it may not be the final treatment. A tooth might need a root canal, a crown or an extraction after the infection has settled. We explain what is next and why it matters.

Please do not stop at the emergency visit. A temporary filling or dressing is just that: temporary. Finishing the treatment protects you from the same pain returning, and it is often cheaper than starting again after a break.

Questions about toothache & emergency care

What can I do at home while I wait for my appointment?

Rinse gently with lukewarm salt water, keep your head slightly raised, and use a cold compress on the cheek if it is swollen. Take pain relief as directed on the pack. Do not put aspirin on the gum.

When is a toothache an emergency?

Facial swelling, fever, trouble swallowing or breathing, or bleeding that will not stop need urgent care. Call us straight away, and go to a hospital emergency department if you have trouble breathing or swallowing.

What if my tooth gets knocked out?

Pick it up by the crown, not the root. Rinse it briefly with milk or water without scrubbing, and try to place it back in the socket or keep it in milk. Call us immediately.

Will I need a root canal for every toothache?

No. Pain can come from decay, a loose filling, gum infection or sensitivity. We diagnose it first, then choose the simplest treatment that works.
