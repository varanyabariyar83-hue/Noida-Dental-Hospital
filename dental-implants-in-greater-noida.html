What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that replaces the root of a missing tooth. After it is placed in the jawbone, the bone grows around it over a few months and holds it firmly. A connector and a custom-made crown are then fitted on top, so the replacement looks, feels and works like a natural tooth.

Because the implant sits in the bone, it does not rely on the neighbouring teeth for support. This is the biggest difference from a traditional bridge, which needs the teeth on either side to be shaped to hold it. An implant can also help keep the jawbone in the area where the tooth was lost, which a bridge or denture does not do in the same way.

Who Is Suitable for an Implant?

Most healthy adults with enough jawbone and healthy gums can be considered. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and untreated gum disease slow healing and raise the risk of failure, so we assess these before planning treatment.

If you have lost bone after a tooth was missing for many years, an implant may still be possible, sometimes with an additional procedure to build the bone. We will explain whether that applies to you after looking at your X-rays. We will also tell you when an implant is not the best option and what to choose instead.

The Implant Journey: From Consultation to Final Crown

  • Consultation and scans: we examine your gums, bite and bone, take X-rays and discuss what you want.
  • Treatment planning: we decide where each implant will go and whether any preparation is needed first, such as treating gum disease.
  • Implant placement: the titanium post is placed under local anaesthesia.
  • Healing: the implant fuses with the bone over a few months, and we review you along the way.
  • Abutment and crown: a connector and a custom crown are fitted and matched to the shade and shape of your teeth.
  • Aftercare: regular cleaning and check-ups keep the gums and bone around the implant healthy.

The total time depends on your bone and how quickly you heal. We explain the timeline during your consultation, so you can plan around it.

Implants, Bridges and Dentures Compared

Each option has a place. A bridge is quicker and avoids surgery, but it needs the neighbouring teeth to be reshaped. A removable denture is usually the most economical, but it can feel less stable. An implant takes longer, leaves the neighbouring teeth untouched and feels the most natural.

The right choice depends on your gums, bone, the teeth next to the gap, your general health and your budget. Our blog article on implants and bridges goes through the trade-offs in more detail. Whichever route you choose, we plan it so it fits with the rest of your treatment.

If the neighbouring teeth have tilted into a gap over the years, there may not be enough space for a new tooth, and the bite may be off. Caring for Your Implant

Implants cannot decay, but the gums around them can become inflamed if plaque builds up. Brush twice a day, clean between the teeth and the implant with floss or small interdental brushes, and keep up with professional cleaning.

Tell us if you smoke, because smoking affects healing and long-term success. Avoid chewing very hard objects, and tell your dentist if you grind your teeth at night. If an implant crown ever feels loose, or the gum around it bleeds or swells, call us early.

Risks, Success and What We Check First

Implants have a high success rate in healthy patients, but like any treatment they carry risks. Infection, slow healing, an implant that does not fuse with the bone and inflammation around the implant can happen. Smoking, poorly controlled diabetes and gum disease raise the risk, which is why we assess them before planning.

Before we recommend an implant, we look at the amount and quality of the bone, the health of the gums, your bite and your general health. We explain the risks in plain language and what we do to reduce them. If an implant is not suitable, we tell you and discuss other options. Honest assessment protects you from treatment that is unlikely to succeed.

How Long Does Implant Treatment Take?

Timelines vary. A straightforward case takes several months from placement to the final crown, because the bone needs time to fuse with the implant. If a tooth must be removed first or the bone needs building up, the process is longer. Gum disease or braces needed beforehand add to the time.

We give you a timeline at the consultation, with the main milestones. You will usually have a handful of visits: planning, placement, one or more reviews, and the final crown fitting. We keep in touch between visits, and you can call us if anything feels wrong during healing.

Questions About Implants

Will people notice? A well-made implant crown looks and feels like a natural tooth. Can I have several teeth replaced? Yes, with a few implants supporting a bridge or several crowns, depending on your case. Can I have an implant straight after an extraction? Sometimes, if the bone and gums are healthy and there is no infection, but not always.

Can I have an MRI or airport scan? Titanium implants do not normally cause problems with these. How do I clean it? As you would your own teeth, with extra care at the gum line.
